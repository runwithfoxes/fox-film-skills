STYLE: Real live-action film footage, shot on grainy 35mm film stock in 1989 on location on a run-down residential street in Brooklyn, New York, during a heatwave. It looks like a gritty, low-budget independent film of that year: raw, real, slightly faded colour, heavy film grain, nothing polished. The hottest afternoon of the year: hard, direct afternoon sun from a clear, bleached white-blue sky, every surface brightly sunlit, sharp black shadows under the stoops, the railings and the people. No grey sky, no overcast, no mist hanging in the air. No sun rays, no lens flare, no glow, no haze effects. The street is real and worn: cracked pavement, peeling paint, rusty fire escapes, black rubbish bags on the kerb, a battered old car. The colour comes from real things: red brick, painted doors, people's bright summer clothes, the white spray of water. Every shot after the first is from very low down, almost on the ground, on a wide-angle lens, looking up, with the brick buildings and fire escapes towering behind, the frame tilted a little off level. The camera is locked off within each shot and cuts straight between shots. The stylized fox character from [Image1] and [Image2] is placed into this real world the way a computer-animated bear is placed into a live-action family film: his face stays exactly as the references, with realistic dense orange fur, real contact shadows on the pavement. He is the only animated thing in the film. Everything and everyone around him is real, photographed for real.

PLACE: A Brooklyn block of red brick brownstones with high front stoops and iron railings. In the middle of the block an open red fire hydrant on the kerb shoots a thick, roaring white jet of water in a high arc across the street. Real local kids about eleven or twelve years old, in shorts and vests, run and jump through the spray, shrieking and laughing; a man in a vest stands in it with his arms out; a woman on the kerb lets it soak her legs. The road around the hydrant is wet and shining. People sit out on the stoops in the sun in the background, in vests and shorts: a woman fans herself with a folded newspaper, an old man has a wet towel over his head, box fans turn in the open windows above. Nobody stands near the hydrant. No readable signs, labels, graffiti or words anywhere.

CHARACTER - THE FOX: One fox, small and slight: the top of his red cap comes only up to the kids' shoulders, and he is thinner than any of them, no bigger than a five-year-old child. Slim build, fluffy orange fur, cream cheeks, heavy-lidded golden yellow eyes, thick orange tail with a cream tip. For the heat he wears a plain white sleeveless vest, a plain red baseball cap with no logo, his two ears standing up through it, dark blue rolled jeans and white high-top trainers. Bored and grumpy in every shot: eyes alert under flat, heavy lids, brows a little low, mouth a flat line. His expression never changes, even when he wants to get into the water. Keep his exact face, fur colour and his small size against the kids the same in every shot. He has exactly two arms and two paws. He stays completely dry in every shot: not one drop of water ever touches him, his fur and his vest are dry from the first frame to the last.

0-3s: High shot looking straight down from a rooftop over the whole Brooklyn block in the heat: the street, the stoops crowded with people sitting out, rubbish bags on the kerb, the white arc of water from the open hydrant across the road with kids running through it, and on the kerb nearby, small, the fox in his red cap and white vest, standing apart from them.

3-7s: Wide shot from low down on the kerb, looking up. The kids leap through the roaring spray in the foreground, soaked and laughing. Behind them on the dry kerb the fox bends down and pulls off his white high-top trainers one at a time and sets them side by side on the kerb, his face bored and grumpy.

7-10s: Close-up at ground level on the edge of the wet road. His bare orange paws step off the dry kerb, past his two white trainers, towards the spray. Water bounces off the road in the background.

10-12.5s: Shot from low down just behind him, the fox small in the centre of the frame with his back to us, the thick white arc of water roaring right over his head and coming down just in front of him, the kids soaked and shrieking either side. He takes two steps forward so the arc is coming down straight onto the spot where he stands. At that exact moment the whole jet cuts out to nothing at once, from the hydrant to the end of the arc, in a split second. Not one drop lands on him. The kids either side stop and look.

12.5-14s: Close-up of the red hydrant at ground level. A real police officer's hand grips a big iron hydrant wrench on the hydrant's nut and finishes turning it. The last water dribbles out of the hydrant and stops.

14-17s: Wide shot from low down, tilted. The street is suddenly quiet. The fox stands in the middle of the wet road, bone dry, barefoot, his trainers on the kerb behind him. All around him the kids, the man in the vest and the woman are soaked through, water pouring off their hair and clothes, their skin shining wet in the hard sun, and they all turn to look at him. He is the only dry thing on the street. Water drips from a fire escape.

17-20s: Close-up of his face from the front, low down, tilted, the cap brim above his eyes, his fur completely dry, wet brick and dripping people soft behind him. He stares, flat and bored, straight into the lens.

SOUND: A hot city street: the roar and hiss of the hydrant, kids shrieking and laughing, water splashing on the road, distant traffic. MUSIC: an original late-1980s hip-hop beat with a heavy drum and bass line, playing loud from a big portable radio on a stoop, never any existing song. The music and the water cut dead at the same moment when the jet stops, and only a few drips and the far-off traffic are left. No dialogue, no narration.

NEGATIVE: Not animated, not a cartoon, not a 3D animated film, no computer-generated people or buildings, no rendered or polished look, no sun rays, no lens flare, no painted background, no game look. Everything except the fox is real, live-action photography. The fox never has wide eyes, never grins, never smiles, never opens his mouth wide, never looks excited or cute. No hoodie. The fox never gets wet: no water on him, no wet fur, no splash on him, no puddle splashing up on him. The jet never sweeps, drops low or sprays sideways across the road. The water does not slow down or weaken before it stops; it stops all at once when he is under it. No police officer anywhere in the film except his hand on the wrench in the close-up of the hydrant. No grey sky, no overcast light. The fox is never as big as the kids. No readable words, signs, labels, graffiti or logos anywhere, nothing written on the cap. No dog, no bird, no other animal. No second fox, no change to his face, no feral animal on all fours, no human hands on the fox, no extra arms or paws, no distorted anatomy, no floating. No close-up of sweat, no drops of sweat on his face.
