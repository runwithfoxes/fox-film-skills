STYLE: Real live-action film footage, shot on grainy 35mm film stock in 1989 on location on a run-down residential street in Brooklyn, New York, at the end of a heatwave day. It looks like a gritty, low-budget independent film of that year: raw, real, slightly faded colour, heavy film grain, nothing polished. Dusk: the sky a deep warm orange fading to blue above the rooftops, the street lights just coming on with an orange sodium glow, the warm yellow light of a take-away window spilling onto the pavement, long soft shadows. No sun rays, no lens flare, no haze effects. The street is real and worn: cracked pavement, rusty fire escapes, black rubbish bags on the kerb, an old car. The colour comes from real things: red brick, the orange street lights, the yellow window, people's summer clothes. Every shot after the first is from very low down, almost on the ground, on a wide-angle lens, looking up, the frame tilted a little off level. The camera is locked off within each shot and cuts straight between shots. The stylized fox character from [Image1] and [Image2] is placed into this real world the way a computer-animated bear is placed into a live-action family film: his face stays exactly as the references, with realistic dense orange fur, real contact shadows on the pavement. He is the only animated thing in the film. Everything and everyone around him is real, photographed for real.

PLACE: The same Brooklyn block of red brick brownstones with high front stoops and iron railings. On the corner, a small take-away place serves hot dogs through one open window in its brick front, lit warm yellow inside, a real cook in a white T-shirt and paper hat handing hot dogs out through it, steam rising off the grill behind him. Above the window, rolled up, is a grey metal roll-down shutter. A short queue of real people waits on the pavement in front of the window: a man in a vest, a woman with a shopping bag, an older man in a cap. Box fans turn in the open windows above, people still sit out on the stoops in the warm evening. No readable signs, labels, menus, graffiti or words anywhere.

CHARACTER - THE FOX: One fox, small and slight: the top of his red cap comes only up to the grown-ups' waists, and he is thinner than any of them. Slim build, fluffy orange fur, cream cheeks, heavy-lidded golden yellow eyes, thick orange tail with a cream tip. He wears a plain white sleeveless vest, a plain red baseball cap with no logo, his two ears standing up through it, dark blue rolled jeans and white high-top trainers. Bored and grumpy in every shot: eyes alert under flat, heavy lids, brows a little low, mouth a flat line. His expression never changes, even when he wants the hot dog. Keep his exact face, fur colour and his small size against the people the same in every shot. He has exactly two arms and two paws. He stands on the pavement in every shot.

0-3s: High shot looking down from a rooftop over the same Brooklyn block at dusk: the street lights coming on, people on the stoops, and on the corner the warm yellow window of the take-away with a short queue on the pavement in front of it, and at the very back of the queue, small, the fox in his red cap and white vest.

3-7s: Wide shot from low down on the pavement, looking up along the queue towards the yellow window. The cook hands a hot dog out to the woman at the front and she walks off eating it. The queue shuffles one step forward. The fox, at the back, shuffles forward with it, his face bored and grumpy.

7-10s: Close-up at ground level of the queue's feet on the cracked pavement in the orange street light: grown-ups' sneakers and sandals, and among them his small white high-tops. The feet in front step forward and his trainers step forward after them.

10-13s: Shot from low down just behind the fox, him small in the centre of the frame with his back to us, the bright yellow window in front of him, the cook inside behind it. The man in front of him takes two hot dogs and walks away out of the frame, and the fox steps up to the front, alone at the window, the warm light on his face and vest.

13-14.5s: Close-up from the side at the window, his face in profile on the left of the frame, the bright window on the right. At that exact moment the grey metal shutter slams down from the top of the window to the sill in one fast drop, with a loud metal crash, right in front of his nose. The yellow light is gone and his face is lit only by the orange street light.

14.5-17.5s: Wide shot from low down, tilted. He stands alone in front of the shut grey shutter, the corner dark now, the street lights orange, the sky deep blue. Behind him the people from the queue walk away along the pavement eating their hot dogs. He has not moved.

17.5-20s: Close-up of his face from the front, low down, tilted, the cap brim above his eyes, the shut grey shutter behind him, orange street light on his fur. He stares, flat and bored, straight into the lens.

SOUND: A warm summer evening on a city street: the sizzle of the grill, people chatting in the queue and on the stoops, distant traffic. MUSIC: an original late-1980s hip-hop beat with a heavy drum and bass line, playing from a big portable radio on a stoop, never any existing song. The music cuts dead on the crash of the shutter, and only the far-off traffic and the buzz of the street lights are left. No dialogue, no narration.

NEGATIVE: Not animated, not a cartoon, not a 3D animated film, no computer-generated people or buildings, no rendered or polished look, no sun rays, no lens flare, no painted background, no game look. Everything except the fox is real, live-action photography. The fox never has wide eyes, never grins, never smiles, never opens his mouth wide, never looks excited or cute. No hoodie. The fox never gets a hot dog and never holds any food. The shutter does not come down slowly or in stages, it drops all at once when he reaches the front. No one pulls the shutter down on screen before it drops. No readable words, signs, labels, menus, graffiti or logos anywhere, nothing written on the shutter, the window or the cap. No dog, no bird, no other animal. No second fox, no change to his face, no feral animal on all fours, no human hands on the fox, no extra arms or paws, no distorted anatomy, no floating. The fox is never as big as the people.
