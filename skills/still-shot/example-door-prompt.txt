STYLE: Photorealistic live-action film footage, shot on 35mm on a real small-town Irish street on a grey winter afternoon. ONE SINGLE CONTINUOUS SHOT for the whole film, no cuts. A plain, observational camera on a tripod that never moves, never pans, never zooms: a long shot from across the street at eye level, framed square-on to the shop front, level and centred, like a dull record of an ordinary afternoon. Flat, sunless overcast light, a pale white sky, washed-out desaturated colour: dull greys, off-whites and wet concrete, grey slush at the kerb. The only strong colour in the whole frame is the fox's orange fur. Slight film grain. The stylized fox character from [Image1] and [Image2] is placed into this real world the way a computer-animated bear is placed into a live-action family film: his face and design stay exactly as the references, with realistic dense orange fur, real contact shadow on the wet pavement. Everything around him is real and ordinary.

PLACE: The front of a small, plain supermarket in dull grey and off-white, with a pair of automatic sliding glass doors in the exact centre of the frame and a small plain grey sensor box above them. Through the glass, a dim interior with plain shelves and nobody inside. A wet grey pavement in front. The street is empty: no people, no cars passing. No readable signs, no lettering on the shop front, no words, no logos anywhere.

CHARACTER - THE FOX: One fox, compact small frame, much shorter than the doors, slim build, fluffy orange fur, cream cheeks, heavy-lidded golden yellow eyes, dark charcoal hoodie, dark blue rolled jeans, brown boots, thick orange tail with a cream tip. Grumpy and unimpressed: eyes alert under flat lids, completely bored expression. Never angry, never shocked. Small in the frame, about a fifth of the frame's height. Keep his exact face, fur colour and size the same the whole film. He has exactly two arms and two paws. He moves slowly and calmly.

THE PIGEON: One real grey city pigeon, walking, never flying.

0-2s: The fox walks in slowly from the left along the pavement and stops right in front of the closed glass doors, facing them.

2-5s: The doors stay shut. He stands completely still, waiting, facing the doors.

5-7s: He takes one slow step back, then one slow step forward to the doors again. The doors stay shut.

7-9s: He lifts one paw and waves it slowly up towards the sensor box above the doors. The doors stay shut. He lowers his paw.

9-11s: A real grey pigeon walks in from the right along the pavement and stops beside him at the doors. The glass doors slide open for the pigeon. The pigeon walks inside.

11-12s: The doors slide shut again just as the fox steps forward, and he stops with his nose at the closed glass.

12-15s: Hold. The fox stands at the shut doors, then slowly turns his head and stares flat and bored straight at the camera across the street. Nothing else moves.

SOUND: Real street sound only: a distant car on a wet road, wind, a shopping trolley rattling somewhere far off, the pigeon's feet, the whir of the door motor and the doors sliding open and shut. Long silences. No music, no dialogue, no narration.
