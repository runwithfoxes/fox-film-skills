STYLE: Photorealistic live-action film footage, shot on 35mm on a real Irish seafront promenade on a grey, windy afternoon. Real overcast daylight, flat and silver, real wet concrete, real grey sea with whitecaps, slight film grain, wide anamorphic frame. The camera is locked off and never moves; it cuts straight between shots. The close-ups are extreme: a single eye filling the frame from edge to edge, shot on a long lens, held perfectly still. The stylized fox character from [Image1] and [Image2] is placed into this real world the way a computer-animated bear is placed into a live-action family film: his face and design stay exactly as the references, with realistic dense orange fur moving in the wind, individual strands catching the grey light, real contact shadows on the bench. Everything around him is real and ordinary. The seagull is a real bird filmed for real, never animated.

PLACE: A plain wooden bench with a metal frame on a concrete seafront promenade, a low sea wall and railing behind it, the grey sea and a flat horizon beyond. Empty promenade, no people. No readable signs, no words anywhere.

CHARACTER - THE FOX: One fox, compact small frame, slim build, fluffy orange fur, cream cheeks, heavy-lidded golden yellow eyes, dark charcoal hoodie, dark blue rolled jeans, brown boots, thick orange tail with a cream tip. He sits on the left half of the bench, on the left of the frame, in every wide and medium shot. Grumpy and unimpressed: eyes alert under flat lids, brows a little low, completely bored expression. Never sleepy, never angry, never shocked. Keep his exact face, fur colour and size the same in every shot. He has exactly two arms and two paws. His body stays seated and calm the whole time; only his head turns and his paw moves where the shots say.

PROP: Half a sandwich, cut into a triangle, white bread with a filling showing at the cut edge, lying on its open, flat white paper wrapper on the bench seat right beside his hip, on his right side, between him and the gull. The sandwich is about the size of his paw. It is the only food in the film.

THE GULL: A real adult herring gull: white head, pale grey back, black wing tips with white spots, yellow beak with a red spot, pale yellow eye with a red ring. It stands on the top of the bench backrest about a metre to his right, on the right of the frame, in every wide and medium shot.

0-3s: Wide shot from the front, the whole bench, the sea wall and the grey sea behind. The fox sits on the bench, paws on his knees, the sandwich on its paper beside his hip. The gull lands on the top of the backrest about a metre to his right and folds its wings.

3-6s: Medium shot from the front, the fox on the left and the gull on the right, the sandwich on the bench between them. The fox slowly turns his head to his right and looks at the gull. The gull turns its head and looks back down at him. They hold each other's look.

6-8s: The same medium shot. The gull looks down at the sandwich, then back up at the fox. The fox keeps looking at the gull.

8-9.5s: Extreme close-up of the fox's eye, golden yellow under a flat lid, filling the frame, looking to the right of the frame towards the gull. It does not blink. Fur moves in the wind.

9.5-11s: Extreme close-up of the gull's pale yellow eye with its red ring, filling the frame, looking to the left of the frame towards the fox. It does not blink.

11-12.5s: Extreme close-up of the half sandwich lying on its flat white paper wrapper on the wooden bench, the wrapper's edge lifting a little in the wind.

12.5-13.5s: Extreme close-up of the fox's eye again. It flicks down towards the sandwich, then back up to the right.

13.5-15s: Extreme close-up of the fox's paw on the bench seat, sliding slowly sideways towards the sandwich on its paper.

15-16s: Extreme close-up of the gull's yellow beak with the red spot, opening a little.

16-17.5s: Medium shot from the front, as before. Both move at once: the fox's paw reaches for the sandwich, and the gull's head darts down first, grabs the sandwich in its beak and flies off out of the frame to the right. The fox's paw lands flat on the empty paper wrapper.

17.5-20s: Hold on the fox alone on the bench, his paw still flat on the empty white wrapper, the wrapper's edge flapping in the wind. He slowly turns his head back to face the camera and stares flat and bored straight into the lens. Nothing else moves.

SOUND: Wind, waves breaking on the sea wall, the gull's wings flapping as it lands, the paper wrapper rustling, the gull's wings flapping away. MUSIC: an original film score in the style of a 1960s Italian western: a lone whistled melody, a twangy electric guitar with reverb, a jaw harp, one distant trumpet, sparse with long silences between the notes, building slowly from the first look to the grab. An original tune, never any existing film theme. The music stops dead the moment the gull grabs the sandwich, and the last shot has only wind. No dialogue, no narration.

NEGATIVE: No cartoon or animated set, no painted background, no game look, everything except the fox is real. The gull is a real bird, not a cartoon bird, it never talks, never has a human expression. Only one gull. No people. No readable words, signs or logos anywhere. No cup, no cone, no chips, no ice cream; the only food is the half sandwich, the size of his paw in every shot. No second fox, no change to his face, no feral animal on all fours, no human hands on the fox, no extra arms or paws, no distorted anatomy, no floating. The camera never moves, never zooms, never shakes. The fox never stands up, never chases the gull, never gets the sandwich. The fox is always on the left of the frame and the gull always on the right.
